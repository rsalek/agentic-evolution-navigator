# Dell describes production agents and portfolio concentration

Dell CTO John Roese said the company has production agents in CRM data cleanup, software engineering, and special-pricing workflows. He described specification-driven coding through CI/CD and said Dell narrowed roughly 900 AI projects to about 13 priority programmes. He also said the programmes helped decouple revenue growth from the cost structure and described agent identities, logging, and a kill switch as governance requirements.

This is material operator testimony about live workflows, but the interview does not provide task counts, active users, measurement periods, accepted-change or defect rates, absolute savings, or attribution. The 900-to-13 portfolio reduction measures prioritisation, not adoption or commercial traction; the cost-structure claim remains unquantified.

## Evidence assessment

- Workflow mechanism: agents operate inside CRM, pricing, and specification-to-code pipelines with identity, audit, and intervention controls.
- Adoption evidence: named production workflow categories, without repeat-volume or outcome denominators.
- Commercial signal: no disclosed agent revenue; the operating-benefit claim is too unbounded to upgrade commercial proof.
- Open questions: active agents and tasks, success/error/reversal rates, accepted code changes, security incidents, absolute savings, customer deployments, and revenue attribution.
