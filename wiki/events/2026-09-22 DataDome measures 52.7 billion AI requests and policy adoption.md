# DataDome measures 52.7 billion AI requests and policy adoption

DataDome's 2026 State of Bot and Agent Security report analyzes anonymized traffic from more than 75,000 customer sites between July 2025 and June 2026, plus tests of 21,491 popular websites against ten bot and spoofed-agent types. It reports 52.7 billion AI-agent and LLM-crawler requests during the 12-month study period. AI traffic grew 82.3%, and bots plus AI agents accounted for about 26.5% of traffic across the measured customer base.

In the first half of 2026, DataDome recorded 605.6 million AI requests to high-risk endpoints including login pages, forms, carts, payment flows, and account-creation pages. Monthly AI requests to login pages rose from 11.9 million in January to 99.7 million in June. AI-agent spoofing increased 45% between February and July 2026. The external website test found 65.3% completely unprotected and only 2.4% fully protected.

This updates DataDome's Q2 report, which measured 17.7 billion AI-agent requests across more than 400 enterprises, up 45% from 12.2 billion in Q1. DataDome also reported that 54% of its customers had adopted agent-trust policies. The policy-adoption share provides a commercial bridge from traffic growth to use of controls inside DataDome's customer base, but the company does not disclose whether the feature carries incremental pricing.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is an emerging monetization green shoot for DataDome's agent-trust and traffic-classification layer.

- Company: DataDome, a private bot and agent-trust provider.
- Payer: an enterprise customer operating protected websites, applications, or APIs under a DataDome platform contract.
- Customer or market-share evidence: 54% of DataDome customers had adopted agent-trust policies in Q2 2026. The later report covers more than 75,000 customer sites but does not disclose the number of paying organizations, net additions, or competitive share.
- Toll-gate mechanism: continuous classification of human, bot, crawler, and AI-agent traffic, followed by identity, intent, access, and protection policies across high-risk endpoints.
- Revenue or economic bridge: the telemetry and policies run within DataDome's commercial platform and its existing customer base. The sources do not isolate agent-trust pricing, paid attach, contract expansion, revenue, ARR, renewal, or margin.
- Measured period: Q2 2026 for customer-policy adoption and quarterly agent traffic; July 2025 through June 2026 for the broader traffic study; February through July 2026 for spoofing growth.
- Baseline and denominator: 17.7 billion Q2 requests versus 12.2 billion in Q1 across more than 400 enterprises; 52.7 billion AI requests across more than 75,000 customer sites over 12 months; 54% of DataDome customers adopting agent-trust policies.
- Repeatability: quarterly and monthly observations show repeated traffic classification, including 11.9 million January login-page requests rising to 99.7 million in June.
- Attribution quality: high for DataDome's own customer-network telemetry and policy configuration; medium for commercial attribution because the company does not separate agent-trust contracts or incremental revenue.
- Still unproven: absolute paying-customer count, paid attach, incremental ACV or ARR, renewal, policy effectiveness, false-positive cost, customer outcomes, margin, and the share of requests generated by action agents rather than crawlers.
- Strongest falsifier: customers enable bundled policies without paying more, renewing because of them, or receiving measurable fraud, cost, conversion, or risk benefits.
- Next verification trigger: agent-trust pricing, paid attach or contract expansion, customer-count growth, renewal, agent-specific ARR or revenue, and independently confirmed outcome metrics.

## Why it matters

The update closes two graph gaps. It supplies a bounded 12-month denominator for AI traffic and shows that agent classification reaches login, account, cart, and payment endpoints. It also supplies a customer-policy adoption share for a commercial trust platform. The evidence remains vendor-measured and combines action agents with LLM crawlers, so it cannot establish market-wide agent activity or incremental revenue on its own.
