# G2 finds agent purchasing and production rise with oversight costs

G2's July 2026 global survey of 1,019 B2B software purchase decision-makers found that 86% had purchased AI-agent software or services during the previous year, up from 69% in the prior study, a 17-percentage-point or approximately 25% relative increase. Sixty-six percent reported agents in limited or scaled production, and nearly half of organisations with completed pilots said most of those pilots had advanced to full production. Among organisations with agents in production, 38% operated more than 100 agents.

The survey also measures a material counterweight. Thirty-one percent said employees spent at least six hours each week overseeing agents, 45% said oversight and rework consumed more than a quarter of expected productivity gains, and 46% reported higher-than-expected operating costs. Thirty-six percent reported an agent-related security incident during the previous year.

## Commercial evidence gate

- **Payer:** the surveyed B2B organisation purchasing agent software or services.
- **Toll-gate mechanism:** vendor-specific software and service purchases; the survey does not disclose pricing model, contract value, or whether payment is seat-, usage-, outcome-, or service-based.
- **Metric, period, and denominator:** 1,019 global B2B purchase decision-makers surveyed in July 2026; purchase incidence increased from 69% to 86% year over year; 66% reported limited or scaled production.
- **Revenue bridge:** the purchase-incidence increase is direct market-level paying-customer evidence, but it does not identify vendors, spend, ARR, realized revenue, renewal, or margin. It is not an investable company-specific conclusion.
- **Repeatability and maturity:** production is defined as live work in actual organisational systems rather than a demonstration or time-limited pilot. The combined limited-or-scaled category prevents a clean split between bounded production and organisation-wide scale.
- **Attribution quality:** high for the survey population, timing, definitions, and reported responses; medium for market-wide extrapolation because purchasing and operating outcomes are self-reported and the sampling frame is not a census of all businesses.
- **Still unproven:** dollars spent, vendor mix, paid seats or usage, contract expansion, renewal, retention, realized revenue, successful outcome volume, correction cost per task, and margin.
- **Strongest falsifier:** vendor filings and independent spend data fail to show comparable paid-customer or revenue growth, or a repeat survey shows that purchases stalled or contracted because oversight and operating costs erased value.
- **Next verification trigger:** a comparable 2027 survey plus vendor-level paid-customer, ARR or ACV, renewal, production-use, oversight-cost, and successful-outcome disclosures.

This is a market-level monetization green shoot and simultaneous economics warning. It strengthens evidence that enterprises are paying for and deploying agents, while challenging any thesis that production automatically creates attractive unit economics or broad autonomy.
