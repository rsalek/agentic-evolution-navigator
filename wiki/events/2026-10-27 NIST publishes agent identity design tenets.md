# NIST publishes agent identity design tenets

The National Institute of Standards and Technology published ten proposed design tenets for agent identity, authentication, and authorization. They cover distinct cryptographically verifiable agent identities; linked, attenuated delegation; zero standing privilege; short-lived and revocable authorization; human-review triggers; signed mandates and constraints; separation of policy from model reasoning; deterministic access decisions; continuous monitoring; and independent tamper-evident evidence.

NIST says the tenets are initial guideposts drawn from public comments and stakeholder engagement. It also says current standards and technology may not immediately satisfy every tenet and plans to use them in future National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence demonstrations. This is standards and control-readiness evidence, not adoption or monetization evidence.

## Commercial evidence gate

No payer, product price, paid deployment, customer count, control volume, prevented loss, revenue, renewal, or margin is disclosed. Potential toll gates include agent identity, delegated authorization, policy enforcement, monitoring, and immutable audit evidence, but NIST does not identify a commercial provider or economic outcome.

The strongest falsifier for the control-demand interpretation would be production evidence showing that existing human and service-account IAM controls already manage agent actions effectively without agent-specific identity, delegation, monitoring, or audit layers. The next verification trigger is a NIST demonstration, standards profile, regulator requirement, named production deployment, or measured control outcome.
