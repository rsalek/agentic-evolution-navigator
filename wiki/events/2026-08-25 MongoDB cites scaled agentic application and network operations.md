# MongoDB cites scaled agentic application and network operations

MongoDB CEO Dev Ittycheria cited Emergent Labs and AT&T to argue that operational data infrastructure becomes more important as agents generate applications and act on live systems. MongoDB's customer material says Emergent users built nearly two million applications in four months, deployed more than 50,000, and doubled the deployment rate from 10% to 20%. Independent reporting based on AT&T executive comments says its AI-supported network system avoided 12 million customer downtime hours and 3.1 million unnecessary field dispatches over the prior year.

The summit transcript incorrectly renders the AT&T metric as "$12 million of downtime saved." The corroborated unit is **12 million customer downtime hours**, not dollars. MongoDB is one component of AT&T's broader data and AI stack, so the operating outcomes cannot be attributed solely to MongoDB. An anonymous frontier-lab migration claim in the interview is excluded from graph conclusions because the customer cannot be verified.

## Evidence assessment

- Workflow mechanism: persistent operational context, retrieval, and state for generated applications and network-assurance workflows.
- Adoption evidence: repeated application builds and deployments, plus independently reported operational outcomes at AT&T.
- Commercial signal: emerging workload demand and customer operating benefit, but no MongoDB-attributed agent revenue, pricing, retention, or margin.
- Open questions: paid MongoDB consumption attributable to these workloads, app retention and active use, AT&T causal attribution, correction rates, and multi-period economics.
