# OpenAI launches persistent dots agents

OpenAI launched dots, persistent agents that have cloud computers, browsers, connected tools, recurring tasks, and access to ChatGPT, email, text messages, and Slack. Dots can delegate work to subagents and continue working between conversations.

The rollout starts with ChatGPT Pro users outside the European Economic Area, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom. Business Premium users can access dots in supported regions, while Enterprise workspaces can enable a beta that is off by default. Usage does not count against eligible plan allowances for the first month. OpenAI says it will publish later usage terms after that period.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is distribution and product-maturity evidence, not a monetization green shoot. Dots are packaged into paid plans, but OpenAI reports no dot-specific price, paying-customer growth, paid attach, active users, retained tasks, usage, incremental revenue, renewal, or margin. The temporary allowance holiday and unpublished later terms prevent a clean usage-pricing bridge.

## Safety evidence

OpenAI delivered 50,000 simulated emails across 100 bulk-attack evaluations, including 16,600 generated attacks, and recorded no scored attack successes. The result is internal evaluation evidence, not a production incident rate. In a separate persistence test, increasing intervening tasks from five to ten raised a reported alignment-failure flag rate from 8.6% to 19.7%. OpenAI also says its production monitor sometimes failed to assign the highest priority to adversarially edited actions. These results make persistence and accumulated context material control variables.

## What remains open

- Active dots, recurring-task volume, accepted outcomes, retention, and human-review effort.
- Usage terms after the introductory month and any incremental revenue or margin.
- Production prompt-injection, unauthorized-action, correction, reversal, and monitoring rates.
- Whether broad ongoing responsibility produces durable value outside bounded workflows.
