# AWS reports AgentCore customer and deployment growth

Amazon's third-quarter 2026 earnings release reports that tens of thousands of customers currently use Amazon Bedrock AgentCore and that they deployed twice as many agents in Q3 as in the prior three quarters combined. AgentCore is a managed infrastructure layer for deploying, connecting, securing, evaluating, and operating agents. The disclosure establishes a large customer denominator and repeated deployment growth, but does not provide the absolute agent count, active workload volume, customer cohort, or paid share.

AWS publishes consumption pricing across AgentCore Runtime, Gateway, Policy, Identity, Memory, evaluations, observability, registry, web search, and other capabilities. Runtime is billed by active CPU and memory or managed instances, Gateway by API operations, Policy by authorization requests and tokens, and several other capabilities by records, retrievals, evaluations, or queries. Some components and free-tier allowances can be used without incremental AgentCore charges.

Amazon also reports that Alexa for Shopping reached more than 400 million customers in the last twelve months and that interactions rose 570% year over year. This is material agent-distribution evidence, but Amazon does not disclose agent-attributed purchases, gross merchandise value, fees, revenue, or margin, so it is not part of the AgentCore commercial classification.

## Commercial evidence gate

This qualifies as an emerging monetization green shoot through customer scale, repeated deployment growth, and a published usage-based toll. Realized AgentCore revenue and the paid share of use remain undisclosed.

- Company: Amazon Web Services.
- Payer: AWS customers that consume AgentCore runtime, gateway, policy, identity, memory, evaluation, observability, registry, search, and related cloud resources.
- Toll-gate mechanism: consumption-based compute, API, authorization, token, memory, record, evaluation, observability, and query charges, plus managed-instance fees and underlying AWS resources.
- Revenue bridge: tens of thousands of customers use AgentCore and deployed twice as many agents in Q3 as in the prior three quarters combined; published pricing maps deployed agent activity to billable resources. Free allowances and bundled capabilities prevent a clean paid-customer inference.
- Measured period: quarter ended September 30, 2026; Q3 deployment volume is compared with the aggregate of the prior three quarters.
- Baseline and repeatability: the prior-three-quarter deployment aggregate is the baseline. Absolute agent count, active-agent count, paid workload volume, and retained cohorts are not disclosed.
- Maturity: scaled.
- Confidence: high for the primary customer, deployment, and pricing disclosures; medium for realized monetization attribution.
- Customer and market-share evidence: tens of thousands of current AgentCore customers; no market share, paid-customer count, or customer-growth rate is disclosed.
- Still unproven: AgentCore revenue, paid attach, paid customer count, absolute active agents, task volume, renewal, retention, customer concentration, gross margin, and cost per successful outcome.
- Strongest falsifier: most reported customers and deployments remain in free, experimental, inactive, or low-spend use and do not produce durable paid consumption or renewal.
- Next verification trigger: AgentCore revenue or ARR, paid-customer and active-agent counts, billable API or runtime volume, cohort retention, workload outcomes, and gross-margin disclosure.
