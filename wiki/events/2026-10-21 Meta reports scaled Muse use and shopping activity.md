# Meta reports scaled Muse use and shopping activity

Meta's Q3 earnings call supplies the first primary operating bridge from Muse's September launch to scaled use and commerce. In roughly six weeks, more than 10 million people in the United States and Canada set up a Muse personal agent, and about 25% of people who set it up used it on a given day. More than a quarter of weekly active users used Muse for shopping, with an average purchase size above $200. Meta said Plaid had not seen comparable connector activity on another service and that Stripe raised credit limits during the first week because volume exceeded expectations.

## Commercial evidence gate

- **Company:** Meta.
- **Payer:** consumers can pay for additional Muse tokens through the already documented monthly tiers; Meta says businesses are intended to pay transaction fees for commerce that Muse facilitates, but it does not say those fees are live or recognized in revenue.
- **Customer and activity evidence:** more than 10 million setups in the United States and Canada over roughly six weeks; approximately 25% daily use among people who set up Muse; shopping by more than 25% of weekly active users.
- **Toll-gate mechanism:** recurring subscriptions for advanced models or additional tokens are live pricing paths. Meta describes future business-paid transaction fees, business subscriptions, and workplace subscriptions as additional tolls.
- **Revenue or economic bridge:** an average purchase size above $200 and a first-week Stripe credit-limit increase indicate repeated value-bearing activity across a live commerce connector. The direct subscription price is documented elsewhere in the graph. Meta discloses no paid-subscriber count, exact purchase count or value, merchant breadth, current fee, realized Muse revenue, refunds, disputes, or margin.
- **Measured period and baseline:** launch through October 21, 2026, approximately one and a half months. The disclosure gives setup and usage ratios but no prior-period baseline, weekly-active denominator, cohort curve, or country split beyond the United States and Canada.
- **Repeatability:** daily-use and weekly-shopping shares imply repeated operation across a large setup base; average purchase size and credit-limit pressure indicate more than a single demonstration. Exact repeat-buyer and repeat-merchant cohorts remain unpublished.
- **Attribution quality:** high for the operating measures because Meta's CEO and CFO disclosed them in an official earnings-call transcript. Attribution is weak for revenue because company-wide advertising, subscription, and Reality Labs results are not separated for Muse.
- **Still unproven:** paid conversion, tier mix, renewal, exact transaction count and value, active merchants, transaction fees, Muse-attributed revenue, refunds and disputes, retained shopping cohorts, serving cost per successful task, and contribution margin.
- **Strongest falsifier:** setup and shopping activity decay after launch, purchases are concentrated in a small subsidized cohort or reversed at high rates, paid conversion and merchant willingness to pay remain low, or inference costs overwhelm subscription and commerce economics.
- **Next verification trigger:** an official disclosure of paid Muse subscribers, tier mix, renewal, transaction count and value, active and retained buyers and merchants, realized fees or agent-attributed revenue, refunds and disputes, and serving cost or contribution margin.

This is an emerging monetization green shoot: the primary source connects a scaled active-agent base to repeated value-bearing shopping and an explicit subscription and future transaction-fee architecture. It is not measured agent revenue.
