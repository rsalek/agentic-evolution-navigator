# Haizhi reports direct Atlas agent revenue and margin growth

Beijing Haizhi Technology Group's unaudited interim results for the six months ended June 30, 2026 report RMB114.6 million of revenue from Atlas Agent, up 135.6% from RMB48.6 million in the prior-year period. Atlas Agent represented 38.8% of group revenue, served 29 customers, and recorded a 52.9% gross margin, up 4.8 percentage points year over year. The company says 69% of existing Atlas Graph-solution customers converted to demand for Atlas Agent.

The issuer's audited 2025 annual report provides a longer baseline: Atlas Agent revenue was RMB145.7 million for 2025, up 68.4%, and its gross margin was 53.2%. Group revenue in the 2026 interim period was RMB295.6 million, up 70.4%; group gross margin increased 6.2 percentage points to 44.7%. The group still reported an RMB79.0 million net loss and an RMB19.6 million adjusted net loss for the half-year.

## Commercial-evidence gate

This qualifies as a **measured monetization green shoot**. It is direct, agent-segment revenue and margin evidence from a listed issuer, with a prior-period baseline, paying-customer denominator, installed-base conversion signal, and repeated multi-period growth.

- **Company:** Beijing Haizhi Technology Group, listed in Hong Kong as 2706.
- **Payer:** The 29 Atlas Agent customers served during the first half of 2026. The filing describes demand across established and newly entered industries but does not disclose customer-level contract values or concentration.
- **Toll-gate mechanism:** Enterprise contracts for Atlas Agent solutions and the associated graph, ontology, harness, deployment, and service layer. The filing does not separate recurring subscription, implementation, license, usage, or services revenue.
- **Revenue bridge:** Atlas Agent revenue increased from RMB48.6 million in the first half of 2025 to RMB114.6 million in the first half of 2026, or 135.6%, and reached 38.8% of group revenue. Segment gross margin rose from 48.1% to 52.9%.
- **Measured period and denominator:** Six months ended June 30, 2026 versus the same period in 2025; 29 Atlas Agent customers; 69% conversion among existing Atlas Graph-solution customers. The 2025 audited annual report supplies an additional full-year revenue and margin checkpoint.
- **Repeatability:** Two consecutive reporting periods show agent-segment growth, and installed-base conversion indicates a repeatable distribution path. Contract recurrence, renewal, backlog, cohort retention, and customer concentration remain undisclosed, so project timing may make revenue lumpy.
- **Attribution quality:** High for Atlas Agent revenue and gross margin because the issuer disaggregates the product line. Lower for total-company margin improvement and future profitability because Atlas Graph, listing costs, research spending, and other factors also contribute.
- **Customer or market-share evidence:** 29 paying customers and 69% installed-base conversion are disclosed. No independent market-share metric is provided.
- **Still unproven:** Recurring versus one-time revenue mix, ACV, backlog, renewal, retention, concentration, cash conversion, deployment duration, action volume, customer-confirmed outcomes, and contribution margin after research, sales, and implementation costs.
- **Strongest falsifier:** A later filing showing that revenue was concentrated in a few one-off projects, that the installed-base conversion does not renew, or that segment margin and cash conversion deteriorate as deployments scale would weaken the conclusion.
- **Next verification trigger:** Full-year 2026 results disclosing Atlas Agent revenue, customer count, gross margin, backlog, repeat versus new customers, contract structure, operating cash flow, and any named customer outcome.

## Cross-event dynamics

The event strengthens the evidence that enterprise-agent monetization can occur in the context, ontology, and harness layer rather than only in model access or transaction rails. The 69% conversion signal also shows how an installed enterprise data platform can distribute an agent product. At the same time, Haizhi's specialist position challenges a narrow view that only large incumbent software suites will capture agent revenue.
