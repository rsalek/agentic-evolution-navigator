# Sensor Tower reports early Muse retention

Yahoo Finance reports Sensor Tower estimates for Meta's Muse September 2026 mobile-install cohort. Muse retained 73.3% of new users on day one, more than 30% on day seven, and 22.5% on day fourteen. ChatGPT's comparable day-one estimate was 41.3%; it overtook Muse between days eight and nine and reached 26.8% on day fourteen. Yahoo separately cites Similarweb estimating 2.182 million Muse daily active users on October 5.

The cohort curve is stronger evidence than an install count because it measures repeated use of the agent. It also supplies counterevidence: the initial advantage decayed quickly and Muse trailed ChatGPT by day fourteen. The figures are third-party estimates reported in secondary coverage; Sensor Tower and Similarweb did not publish an accessible methodology, cohort size, geography, app-versus-web coverage, or paid-user split with the cited numbers.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is an emerging retention and demand green shoot for Meta's agent subscription funnel, not evidence of realized paid monetization.

- Company: Meta, a listed consumer technology and advertising platform.
- Source: [Yahoo Finance's report of Sensor Tower and Similarweb estimates](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/metas-muse-ai-agent-brings-back-73-of-new-users--nearly-double-chatgpts-rate-164557360.html).
- Metric and period: Sensor Tower's September 2026 new-install cohort retained 73.3% on day one, more than 30% on day seven, and 22.5% on day fourteen; Similarweb estimated 2.182 million daily active users on October 5.
- Maturity: scaled usage evidence because the product has a multi-day retention curve and a seven-figure daily-active-user estimate; commercial proof remains emerging.
- Confidence: medium because the estimates are attributed to independent measurement operators but are reported second-hand without the underlying methodology or denominator.
- Payer: a consumer who moves beyond Muse's free tier to Meta's documented Power or Maximum subscription.
- Customer or market-share evidence: the retention and daily-active-user estimates show repeat demand, not paying-customer growth or commercial market share. Meta discloses no subscriber count or paid conversion.
- Toll-gate mechanism: recurring Muse subscriptions priced at $20 per month for Power and $100 per month for Maximum, with weekly token allowances and monthly renewal.
- Explicit revenue or economic bridge: retained agent users form the conversion pool for Meta's documented recurring subscription tiers. The source does not show that retained users pay, exhaust the free tier, transact, or generate incremental advertising or commerce revenue.
- Baseline or denominator: comparisons are against ChatGPT's reported retention curve, but the Muse cohort size, geography, acquisition source, promotional exposure, and paid/free mix are absent.
- Repeatability: one September install cohort is tracked through fourteen days, plus one October 5 daily-active-user snapshot; no second cohort or renewal period is available.
- Attribution quality: medium for product-level engagement because Muse is the measured application; low for monetization because no paid-user or revenue attribution is disclosed.
- Still unproven: paying subscribers, conversion by tier, monthly renewal, churn beyond fourteen days, retained task volume, transaction count or value, revenue, customer-acquisition cost, inference cost, and contribution margin.
- Strongest falsifier: Muse's retention falls below ChatGPT after the launch window, Meta's cross-promotion and free allowance produce activity without paid conversion, or high inference and support costs make the subscription funnel uneconomic.
- Next verification trigger: Meta-reported paid subscribers, free-to-paid conversion, tier mix, monthly renewal and churn, retained task volume, agent-attributed revenue, transaction value, and contribution margin.

## Why it matters

The update closes part of the adoption gap left by traffic and download measures: users returned to the agent over a measured two-week period. It strengthens the incumbent-distribution thesis while also showing that launch-day advantage is not durable by itself. For public-equity triage, Meta advances from a pricing-only watch item to a retention-backed watch item, but remains exposure- and valuation-gated until paid conversion and economics are visible.
