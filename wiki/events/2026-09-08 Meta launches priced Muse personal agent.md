# Meta launches priced Muse personal agent

Meta announced the U.S. rollout of Muse on iOS, Android, the web, and WhatsApp. Muse can browse, use connected applications, send email, book travel, fill forms, negotiate, continue working in the background, and request approval before sensitive actions. It can purchase through Stripe Link using one-time-use cards and Link purchase protections; Shop Pay and 1Password support are planned.

Meta's primary help documentation now confirms a limited free tier, **Power at $20 per month for 500 million Muse tokens per week**, and **Maximum at $100 per month for 3 billion tokens per week**. The subscriptions auto-renew monthly and remain in limited testing. No paid-subscriber, conversion, usage, retention, task-completion, purchase, or revenue metric is disclosed.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is an announcement-stage pricing green shoot, not evidence of realized monetization or adoption.

- Company: Meta.
- Payer: a U.S. consumer who exhausts free usage and chooses a Muse subscription.
- Customer or market-share evidence: none. Meta provides no registered, active, paying, converted, or retained user count for Muse.
- Toll-gate mechanism: monthly subscription access to additional agent usage, with Stripe Link providing a separate transaction and purchase-protection path.
- Revenue or economic bridge: explicit $20 and $100 monthly subscription prices create a direct consumer revenue mechanism; no subscriptions sold, revenue recognized, commerce value, Link fee, or attach is reported.
- Measured period: launch on September 8, 2026; no operating measurement period is provided.
- Baseline and repeatability: the paid tiers disclose weekly token allowances and monthly renewal, but no free-limit denominator, task allowance, paid conversion, repeated-task, or cohort data.
- Attribution quality: high for subscriptions, exact pricing, token allowances, renewal, and the action/payment architecture because Meta now documents them directly.
- Still unproven: paid users, conversion, usage by tier, retention, completion and correction rates, purchase frequency and value, support and inference costs, gross margin, and any effect on advertising or commerce revenue.
- Strongest falsifier: most users remain on the free tier while low completion quality, high inference cost, or trust concerns prevent conversion and retention.
- Next verification trigger: official plan documentation followed by paid-subscriber, tier mix, retained task volume, transaction value, revenue, and contribution-margin disclosure.

## Why it matters

Muse combines incumbent consumer distribution, long-running action execution, a direct subscription toll, and delegated payment in one product. It strengthens the distribution and recommendation-to-execution dynamics, but the graph should not treat launch availability as evidence of use.
