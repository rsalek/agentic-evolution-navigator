# PYMNTS and Visa find merchant agent readiness lags expectations

The 2026 Global Digital Shopping Index Merchant Edition surveyed 1,185 retail merchants in the United States, Brazil, and the United Arab Emirates in March 2026. The country samples were weighted and stratified to represent small, medium, and large merchants on nationally relevant scales.

The study reports that 11% of small and medium businesses and 19% of large merchants qualify as agent-ready. Only 15% of all surveyed merchants have structured product data that agents can access. A separate analysis of the same study reports that 23% can identify both AI-driven traffic and purchases. Expectations are much higher than current capability. Sixty-eight percent expect agents to account for at least 5% of digital sales within two years, and 38% expect more than 15%.

This is merchant-reported infrastructure and expectation data. It does not measure observed agent transaction volume, value, conversion, repeat use, payment fees, fraud, or revenue. The evidence shows a merchant-readiness and attribution gap rather than current scaled agent commerce.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is not a monetization green shoot.

- Payer: no buyer of agent infrastructure is identified.
- Toll-gate mechanism: structured product data, traffic attribution, checkout, payment, and fraud controls are potential merchant toll points, not measured paid products in this study.
- Revenue bridge: none. Expected future sales share is stated intent rather than agent-attributed revenue.
- Measured period and denominator: 1,185 merchants across three countries surveyed in March 2026.
- Repeatability: the Global Digital Shopping Index is a recurring research series, but this disclosure does not provide a repeated agent-readiness time series.
- Attribution quality: high for the stated survey results and low for actual agent commerce because the study relies on merchant responses rather than transaction telemetry.
- Still unproven: active agents, participating merchants, agent-originated sales, transaction volume and value, conversion, payment choice, fees, fraud, disputes, and retention.
- Strongest falsifier: transaction telemetry showing that agents already generate material repeated sales despite low merchant-reported readiness and attribution capability.
- Next verification trigger: network or merchant telemetry that connects identified agent traffic to completed purchases, transaction value, payment choice, fraud, fees, and retained merchant participation.
