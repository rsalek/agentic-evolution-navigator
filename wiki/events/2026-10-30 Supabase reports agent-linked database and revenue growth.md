# Supabase reports agent-linked database and revenue growth

Supabase co-founder and CEO Paul Copplestone said that registered users increased from about 3 million to 13 million over twelve months and that 2026 revenue would reach four times the prior-year level. He also reported more than one million new databases per week and nearly 50 million databases under management, up from 8 million one year earlier.

The interview links this growth to agent-driven software creation but does not isolate the revenue contribution. Y Combinator described about 70% of new activity as agent-originated, and Copplestone agreed that 2026 activity had shifted sharply toward agents. Supabase's June financing disclosure provides a firmer denominator. It reports 600% annual growth in database launches, with AI tools creating more than 60% of new databases. The October Turso acquisition announcement repeats the weekly database figure and names Superhuman, Sauna.ai, CTO.new, and Mastra as examples of customers using database-per-agent infrastructure.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is an emerging private-company monetization green shoot. The operator reports company revenue growth and measured agent-linked demand, but not agent-attributed revenue.

- Company: Supabase.
- Payer: developers, startups, platforms, and enterprises that pay for managed databases and related Supabase services. The disclosures do not identify paying-customer count or revenue by customer type.
- Toll-gate mechanism: managed database projects, compute, storage, platform services, and high-volume database provisioning. Supabase for Platforms was the company's fastest-growing product in the June disclosure.
- Revenue bridge: company revenue is expected to reach four times the prior-year level while AI tools create more than 60% of new databases and agents account for about 70% of new activity in the October interview. The sources do not quantify how much revenue came from those databases or activities.
- Measured period and baseline: the user, database, and revenue comparisons cover about twelve months. Registered users rose from about 3 million to 13 million, databases under management rose from 8 million to nearly 50 million, and annual revenue is expected to reach four times the prior-year level.
- Repeatability: Supabase reports more than one million new databases each week and repeats that measure across June and October disclosures. The company does not publish retained cohorts, active databases, paid conversion, renewal, or workload depth.
- Attribution quality: high for the company-level operating and revenue measures, and medium for the agent bridge. The 60% database-launch share is a direct company measure. The 70% activity share was stated by the interviewer and affirmed by the CEO without a published methodology.
- Still unproven: agent-attributed revenue, paying-customer growth, price or usage mix, conversion, retention, net revenue retention, customer concentration, gross margin, infrastructure cost per active database, and recognized revenue.
- Strongest falsifier: evidence that most agent-created databases are free, short-lived prototypes with low paid conversion or retention, or that the revenue increase came mainly from non-agent customers and financing-driven expansion.
- Next verification trigger: agent-attributed ARR or recognized revenue, paid conversion by creation channel, active and retained database cohorts, Supabase for Platforms contract value, net retention, and gross margin by workload.

## Private-market posture

Supabase is private. Its June 2026 Series F raised $500 million at a $10 billion pre-money valuation. The reviewed sources do not establish a current secondary-market route, eligible share class, cap table, or terms for qualified investors.
