# HUMAN measures tripling agent traffic and Muse dominance

HUMAN Security reports that agentic request volume observed across the Human Defense Platform rose 209.7% in September 2026 from August, the largest monthly increase since its tracking began. Meta's Muse accounted for 40.34% of monthly agentic traffic after HUMAN gained dedicated detection visibility on September 22. Muse averaged millions of requests per day by month end, while ChatGPT Agent reached 28.6% and Codex products combined reached 11% of the measured traffic.

The activity was concentrated in commerce. Ecommerce sites received 60.3% of all measured agentic traffic and 76.5% of Muse traffic. Search and product routes accounted for 84.3% of Muse requests, while checkout and payment routes accounted for 2.6%. These are route requests, not completed orders or payments. HUMAN says its Sightline product blocks Muse by default and lets each customer choose whether to allow it.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is scaled agent-traffic and trust-control evidence, but not a monetization green shoot.

- Company: HUMAN Security, a private cybersecurity and traffic-governance provider.
- Payer: in principle, an enterprise customer using HUMAN's Sightline Cyberfraud Defense and AgenticTrust controls; the source names no customer buying or expanding specifically because of Muse or agent traffic.
- Customer or market-share evidence: Muse captured 40.34% of September agentic request traffic on HUMAN's measurement surface. This is traffic share within one provider's network, not HUMAN's commercial market share or a paying-customer growth measure.
- Toll-gate mechanism: agent detection, verification, traffic classification, default blocking, and customer-specific allow or deny policy within a commercial security platform.
- Revenue or economic bridge: the traffic and controls operate inside HUMAN's commercial platform, but the source discloses no separate AgenticTrust price, paid attach, contract expansion, ARR, revenue, renewal, fraud reduction, conversion improvement, or margin.
- Measured period: September 2026 versus August 2026, with dedicated Muse visibility from September 22 through month end.
- Baseline and denominator: total HUMAN-observed agentic requests rose 209.7% month over month; Muse represented 40.34% of September traffic and averaged millions of daily requests by month end. HUMAN does not disclose the absolute total, customer or domain count, geographic coverage, or share of the wider web.
- Repeatability: HUMAN publishes a monthly series and provides daily observations for Muse, but only one post-launch month is available for Muse.
- Attribution quality: high for first-party traffic measured on the Human Defense Platform; medium for broader adoption because the source does not disclose coverage, classifier precision and recall, or independent validation.
- Still unproven: completed purchases or payments, conversion, retained Muse usage, verified identity coverage, policy effectiveness, paid attach, revenue, renewal, customer outcomes, and unit economics.
- Strongest falsifier: Muse's launch spike proves temporary, HUMAN's detection or customer mix overstates broader adoption, or the traffic remains research and browsing activity without completed transactions or willingness to pay.
- Next verification trigger: October and subsequent retained traffic, absolute request and customer denominators, classifier performance, allowed-versus-blocked outcomes, completed order or payment count and value, and HUMAN pricing, paid attach, expansion, renewal, or agent-specific revenue.

## Why it matters

The finding closes an important scale gap left by Cequence's denominator-free observation. It shows rapid, repeated agent activity on a named security platform and connects most of that activity to ecommerce routes. It also opens a sharper conversion gap: measurable checkout and payment requests are not evidence of completed commerce, transaction value, or revenue.
