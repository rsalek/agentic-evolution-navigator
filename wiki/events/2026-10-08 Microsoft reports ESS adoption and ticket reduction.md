# Microsoft reports ESS adoption and ticket reduction

Microsoft says its Employee Self-Service Agent was launched to more than 300,000 employees and contingent staff in fall 2025. After early adoption was weaker than expected, internal research found that users distrusted dead ends, repeated troubleshooting, missing context, and unclear escalation. Microsoft redesigned the support journey around context, stated limitations, and handoff to a live agent.

Microsoft reports that 50% of employees now start their support journey with the agent, up from 27% before the trust-building initiative, and that overall IT support tickets created fell 30%. The source does not disclose absolute users, interactions, completed resolutions, the measurement window, or a control group.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is material operating and counterevidence, not a monetization green shoot.

- **Beneficiary and payer:** Microsoft's internal employee-support operation; no external payer or customer contract is identified.
- **Toll gate and revenue bridge:** none. The workflow uses Microsoft's agent stack internally, but the source reports no price, paid attach, contract expansion, renewal, or agent-attributed revenue.
- **Metric, period, and baseline:** first-start share rose from 27% to 50%, a 23-percentage-point increase, while ticket creation fell 30%. The deployment population exceeds 300,000, but the active-user and interaction denominators and measurement window are absent.
- **Repeatability and attribution:** the before-and-after comparison follows a specific trust-design intervention and shows that adoption can improve after product changes. It does not isolate agent quality from channel redesign, communications, workflow changes, or ticket reclassification.
- **Still unproven:** active users, repeat-use cohorts, completed resolutions, autonomous action share, escalation and recovery rates, service quality, labour savings, cost per successful resolution, external paid adoption, and product revenue.
- **Strongest falsifier:** the ticket reduction reflects channel shift, abandoned requests, or deferred human work rather than successful agent resolution, or usage falls after the measured intervention period.
- **Next trigger:** a multi-period operator report with absolute interaction and user counts, repeat use, autonomous completion, escalation and recovery, quality, cost per resolution, and customer-side paid deployment evidence.
