# BeInCrypto finds x402 value concentrated in sub-cent payments

BeInCrypto Research reports that a **35-day x402 sample spanning late July and August 2026 contained 6.4 million payments worth $119,947 in aggregate**. **90.8%** of sampled payments were below one cent. Solana carried **99.2%** of the transactions, and an eight-day burst accounted for **94.5%** of sampled activity.

The repeated payment count shows that machine-compatible settlement is operating at scale, but the value and concentration measures challenge any inference that raw transaction count already represents broad commercial demand. The public research summary also reports that only six of 12 reviewed institutional workflows could initiate or settle payments within authorized limits, while most agents still required human approval to move funds.

## Commercial evidence gate

This is material counterevidence, not a new monetization green shoot.

- Payer: operators of the wallets making x402 payments; the public materials do not disclose verified active agents, businesses, or payer cohorts.
- Toll-gate mechanism: machine-readable, per-request x402 settlement for digital resources such as data, search, inference, compute, and secondary-agent access.
- Revenue or economic bridge: $119,947 reached pay-per-request services during the sampled period, but seller revenue, facilitator fees, take rate, refunds, and margin are not disclosed. Agent attribution is not independently established for every payment.
- Measured period and denominator: 6.4 million payments over 35 days in late July and August 2026; the public summary does not disclose the sampled facilitator set, active-wallet denominator, or wash-activity filters.
- Repeatability: payment activity recurred across the window, but 94.5% of transactions occurred during one eight-day Solana burst, weakening breadth and persistence claims.
- Attribution quality: medium-to-low. The research is original and the topline sample is bounded, but the full methodology is registration-gated and the public summary does not identify active payers or merchants.
- Still unproven: unique active agents, repeat payer and merchant cohorts, retained transaction value, completed business purchases, facilitator revenue, seller economics, disputes, fraud, and customer retention.
- Strongest falsifier: a reproducible, wash-filtered monthly series shows a broad and retained payer and merchant base, rising value per active participant, and realized service or facilitator revenue.
- Next verification trigger: public wallet- and merchant-level cohort statistics with concentration filters, verified agent attribution, transaction count and value, paid-service mix, fees, disputes, and multi-month retention.

## Why it matters

The finding independently reinforces the graph's existing warning that protocol counters and economic adoption are different measurement surfaces. It supports production operation of the rail while challenging the scale and breadth implied by headline transaction totals.
