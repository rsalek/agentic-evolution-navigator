# Intel outlines heterogeneous agentic AI architecture

Intel executives described agentic workloads as a systems problem spanning model routing, orchestration, sandboxing, verification, memory, networking, and accelerators. They argued that CPUs remain important for control-plane and serial work while open and frontier models should be routed across hybrid infrastructure according to cost, privacy, and capability. Intel's Hot Chips and Computex material independently confirms the described architectures and product direction.

The evidence establishes product readiness and a plausible workflow mechanism. It does not identify a paid agent deployment, customer outcome, agent-attributed demand, pricing, revenue, retention, or margin.

## Evidence assessment

- Workflow mechanism: a router or harness places planning, inference, execution, sandboxing, and verification across heterogeneous compute.
- Adoption evidence: none beyond product and architecture availability.
- Commercial signal: broader AI-infrastructure demand, without an attributable agent revenue bridge.
- Open questions: named production deployments, agent workload share, wall-clock and cost-per-success benchmarks, paid attach, revenue, and margin.
