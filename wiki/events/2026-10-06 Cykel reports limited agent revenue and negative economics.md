# Cykel reports limited agent revenue and negative economics

Cykel AI's unaudited interim results report £4,360 of revenue for the six months ended 31 July 2026, compared with £4,107 in the prior-year period. The company says its recruitment agent Lucy, research agent Samson, and sales agent Eve continued to generate revenue as it advanced early-stage commercialisation. Revenue therefore grew by 6.2%, but Cykel describes the level as modest and below that required to cover operating costs.

The same statement reports £8,418 of cost of sales, a £4,058 gross loss, a £1,350,060 operating loss, £136,135 of cash, and negative total equity of £2,068,664. The disclosure is unusually direct evidence that agent products can produce recurring-period revenue without establishing viable product economics.

## Commercial evidence gate

This qualifies as an emerging monetization green shoot under the agent-attributed revenue test, but it is economically adverse rather than an investable proof point.

- Company: Cykel AI PLC.
- Payer: unnamed customers of Cykel's recruitment, research, and sales agents. Customer count and concentration are undisclosed.
- Toll-gate mechanism: paid access or services through Cykel's AI Agent platform. Product pricing, contract form, and revenue allocation are undisclosed.
- Revenue bridge: Cykel explicitly attributes continuing revenue generation to its AI products and reports £4,360 of group revenue for the six-month period.
- Measured period: six months ended 31 July 2026, compared with the same period in 2025.
- Baseline or denominator: £4,107 of prior-period revenue, producing 6.2% growth. No customer, seat, task, or usage denominator is provided.
- Repeatability: revenue appears in two comparable six-month periods, but recurring mix, renewal, and cohort retention are not reported.
- Attribution quality: high for the filed group figures and management attribution, but weaker at product level because Cykel does not split revenue among Lucy, Samson, Eve, or other activities.
- Still unproven: paying-customer count, ARR, ACV, backlog, pricing, attach, renewal, retention, usage, product mix, gross-margin path, concentration, and cash conversion.
- Strongest falsifier: revenue remains below direct costs, operating losses exceed revenue by orders of magnitude, and negative equity indicates that continued product revenue has not established economic viability.
- Next verification trigger: named paid deployments, product-level revenue, customer and renewal counts, contract value, gross-margin progression, and evidence that revenue can scale faster than operating cost.
