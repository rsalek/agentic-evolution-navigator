# Agent-originated traffic is becoming an addressable market

Web infrastructure is beginning to distinguish legitimate agents from undifferentiated bots, creating a basis for access policy, attribution, paid APIs, transaction fees, and agent-specific service tiers.

## Supporting evidence

- [[wiki/events/2026-08-04 Cloudflare announces Wallets for agentic commerce|Cloudflare announces Wallets for agentic commerce]] adds a provider-side mechanism for agent identity, delegated spending, and paid API/content access, but not live traffic or revenue.

- [[wiki/events/2026-07-14 AWS recognizes legitimate AI-agent traffic]] provides an infrastructure mechanism for classification.
- [[wiki/events/2026-07-16 DataDome reports 45 percent Q2 agent-traffic growth]] provides an issuer-reported growth signal.
- [[wiki/events/2026-06-09 Cloudflare presents Web Bot Auth for autonomous commerce]] adds a cryptographic identity and authorization mechanism, but not live traffic or monetization evidence.
- [[wiki/events/2026-07-28 Salesforce reports agentic search becomes first commerce journey step]] adds primary vendor telemetry across more than 1.5 billion shoppers, with AI-chat referral traffic growing 150% to 428% year over year in each measured quarter and agentic search's first-step use growing 200% year over year.
- [[wiki/events/2026-08-07 Cloudflare reports scaled Precursor monitoring and hybrid agentic traffic|Cloudflare reports scaled Precursor monitoring and hybrid agentic traffic]] adds operator telemetry from 206 million security-evaluation events across 73,438 zones and direct observation of human-to-agent session shifts, while leaving the agent-specific denominator and economics open.
- [[wiki/events/2026-06-10 Fastly reports AI request growth and Skyfire agent-commerce integration|Fastly reports AI request growth and Skyfire agent-commerce integration]] adds a primary five-month traffic-growth comparison and an edge identity/payment path, while leaving the agent-only denominator, paid deployment, and economics open.
- [[wiki/events/2026-05-21 Cisco measures 450 percent more network traffic per agent task|Cisco measures 450 percent more network traffic per agent task]] adds a primary controlled-workflow measurement of agent-triggered network intensity and inference dependence, while leaving market-wide traffic share and willingness to pay open.
- [[wiki/events/2026-08-13 OtterlyAI prices Agent Analytics within paid monitoring plans|OtterlyAI prices Agent Analytics within paid monitoring plans]] adds a direct subscription and capacity mechanism for classifying and monitoring agent-originated traffic. It strengthens addressability at the observability layer, while connected-domain scale, repeat usage, customer outcomes, and revenue remain unproven.
- [[wiki/queries/Dageno-surfaced private agentic company watchlist|Dageno-surfaced private agentic company watchlist]] adds a private-company layer around agent-mediated discovery and delivery: Peec AI, Profound, Gauge, AthenaHQ, and Scrunch sell visibility, attribution, or AI-ready serving mechanisms. Their evidence supports addressability but remains distinct from agent-payment or agent-originated transaction revenue.
- [[wiki/events/2026-09-01 Fingerprint reports ARR customer growth and agent detection|Fingerprint reports ARR, customer growth, and agent detection]] adds a production classification layer for authorized agent traffic, backed by platform-level recurring revenue, customer growth, and daily API activity. Agent-specific traffic share and revenue remain undisclosed.
- [[wiki/events/2026-09-22 DataDome measures 52.7 billion AI requests and policy adoption|DataDome's 2026 report]] adds a 12-month, 75,000-site observation frame, 52.7 billion AI requests, 605.6 million H1 requests to high-risk endpoints, and 54% customer adoption of agent-trust policies. It strengthens traffic addressability while leaving action-agent share, conversion, outcomes, and incremental revenue unresolved.
- [[wiki/events/2026-09-30 Cloudflare reports 1700 percent agent-traffic growth|Cloudflare's network telemetry]] adds more than 1,700% year-over-year growth in daily agent requests and a beta path for per-request, per-query, or per-token payment. The growth measure lacks an absolute baseline, and Cloudflare discloses no external paying customer or product economics.
- [[wiki/events/2026-09-30 Cloudflare Monetization Gateway enters production beta|Cloudflare's production beta]] names four live use cases that gate agent-facing resources behind fixed or variable per-request payment. This converts addressable traffic into a seller-revenue path, but gives no traffic-to-payment conversion, active-agent, transaction-value, retention, or Cloudflare revenue measure.
- [[wiki/events/2026-06-30 OpenRouter reports agentic tokens surpass human traffic|OpenRouter]] adds a metered inference path where agentic keys consume about 15 times more tokens per request and token volume exceeded human keys. Published per-token prices and platform fees create a revenue bridge, but agent-attributed spend, paying-key growth, retention, and margin remain unreported.
- [[wiki/events/2026-10-07 Cequence observes undisclosed Muse traffic across customers|Cequence's Muse telemetry]] adds a two-week production observation across multiple customer environments: traffic matching Muse appeared at more than half of the customers studied even though it presented as ordinary Chrome traffic.
- [[wiki/events/2026-10-05 HUMAN measures tripling agent traffic and Muse dominance|HUMAN's September telemetry]] adds a scaled monthly comparison: agentic requests rose 209.7% from August, Muse captured 40.34% of measured agent traffic, and ecommerce received 60.3% of all agentic requests on the platform.
- [[wiki/events/2026-10-15 DataDome measures Q3 agent and Muse traffic|DataDome's Q3 report]] adds nearly 25 billion AI-agent requests, 41.5% quarterly growth, and a 4.7-fold September increase in agentic-browser traffic. The increase remained threefold after excluding Muse, which reduces the risk that one product launch explains the category shift.
- [[wiki/events/2026-10-13 Forter reports agentic order growth and launches commerce engine|Forter's order telemetry]] moves the addressability signal downstream from requests to purchases: a $223 average order, repeated order growth, multi-agent store penetration, and identity linkage for nearly 98% of observed agentic orders on a commercial merchant network.
- [[wiki/events/2026-10-21 Arkose measures agent identity and customer reach|Arkose's Q3 production telemetry]] adds a bounded identity and reach denominator: 65.2% of customers saw named agents on protected flows by September 30, while only 2.3% of legitimate agent sessions carried verified disclosure.

## Challenging evidence

- Traffic growth does not establish willingness to pay, incremental demand, or positive serving economics.
- Salesforce's commerce report does not disclose conversion, order value, repeat purchase, payer, pricing, or Salesforce agent-attributed revenue; it strengthens addressability but not monetization.
- Cloudflare's Precursor snapshot establishes monitoring scale and hybrid traffic behavior, but does not disclose agent traffic share, customer expansion, pricing, or agent-attributed revenue.
- Fastly's traffic category combines agents with crawlers, bots, API systems, and other machine interactions; its 30% AI-request growth and near-half machine-traffic statement therefore do not establish agent-specific demand or willingness to pay.
- Cisco's 450% result comes from one controlled task rather than customer network telemetry; it shows potential network intensity but does not establish broad agent adoption, incremental demand, conversion, or willingness to pay.
- OtterlyAI's paid event capacity establishes a toll gate but not realized willingness to pay; plan inclusion may be bundled, and the company discloses no paid usage, retention, or agent-attributed revenue.
- The Dageno-surfaced companies mostly measure or influence AI discovery rather than prove agent-mediated purchases. Peec AI's reported $4M+ ARR and customer growth are strong company-level signals, but agent-originated revenue remains unallocated; Profound's enterprise scale and Agent credits likewise lack agent-specific ARR and retention.
- Fingerprint does not disclose the share of its 80 million daily API events generated by authorized agents, nor whether agent classification drives incremental spend, attach, or renewal.
- DataDome combines action agents with LLM crawlers and reports vendor-observed traffic within its own customer base. The 54% policy-adoption share does not establish separate pricing, contract expansion, conversion, or positive serving economics.
- [[wiki/events/2026-09-24 AudioEye measures accessibility effect on agent tasks|AudioEye's controlled benchmark]] shows that identifiable agent traffic may still fail to act. Task completion fell from 96% to 31% on the largest accessible-versus-inaccessible comparison, while inaccessible versions used 43% more median tokens across 1,560 runs. The study does not measure live conversion, paid traffic, or revenue.
- Cloudflare says agent traffic can be economically extractive. Some heavily crawled categories saw human traffic fall by as much as 40% in less than one year, while revenue per request fell and serving costs rose. The beta payment rails have not yet shown that paid agent access offsets those losses.
- Cequence does not disclose the number of customers or requests studied, traffic share, classifier performance, or independent validation. The observation therefore supports addressability while leaving the size and accuracy of the measured agent population unresolved.
- HUMAN's shares are provider-surface measures without a customer, domain, geographic, or wider-web denominator. Its 2.6% Muse share on checkout and payment routes measures requests, not completed orders, payments, transaction value, conversion, or revenue.
- DataDome's Muse share differs from HUMAN's because one denominator is agentic-browser traffic and the other is wider agentic traffic on a different provider network. Neither report discloses completed-action value, independent classifier validation, or a direct revenue bridge.
- Forter does not disclose absolute orders, merchants, classifier performance, conversion versus human sessions, reversals, paid attach, or agent-attributed revenue. Its network data establish an addressable transaction surface but not market size or incremental economics.
- Arkose reports broad customer reach but agent traffic remained roughly one in every 5,200 protected sessions. It publishes no raw counts, independent classifier validation, paid attach, customer outcome, or revenue, and its traffic was concentrated among technology, SaaS, and telecommunications customers.

## What would change the thesis

- Verified conversion, attributed revenue, paid access, or repeat agent-customer metrics would strengthen it.
- Predominantly extractive traffic with weak conversion and rising serving costs would weaken it.
