# Agentic depth is separating frontier firms from the median

AI use is broadening across occupations and organizations, but the deepest delegation is concentrating among frontier enterprise users. The emerging divide is not access alone; it is how far organizations embed agentic tools into repeatable work.

## Supporting evidence

- [[wiki/events/2026-05-06 OpenAI reports 16x Codex usage gap]] finds the largest frontier-versus-median usage gap in Codex, an agentic developer tool, and a 3.5x broader token-per-worker gap.
- [[wiki/events/2026-05-05 Microsoft reports 15x active-agent growth]] shows rapid year-over-year growth in active agent objects inside Microsoft 365 and SharePoint.
- [[wiki/events/2026-04-30 Delivery Hero scales autonomous software deployment]] provides named operator evidence of repeated autonomous software deployment outside the provider-level aggregates.
- [[wiki/events/2026-08-25 Snowflake reports company-wide use of an internal sales agent|Snowflake's internal sales agent]] adds a company-reported 4,000-user deployment, while [[wiki/events/2026-08-25 MongoDB cites scaled agentic application and network operations|MongoDB's customer examples]] add repeated application deployment and network-operating outcomes. Both still lack comparable task quality, correction, and vendor-revenue attribution.
- [[wiki/events/2026-08-27 Uber reports scaled agent use and falling unit costs|Uber's software-factory telemetry]] shows a frontier operator with more than 3,600 agent skills, over 30,000 daily executions, sevenfold weekly-user growth, 9.4-fold request growth, and explicit unit-cost optimization.
- [[wiki/events/2026-09-02 Snowflake links agent adoption to AI revenue acceleration|Snowflake's Q2 FY2027 disclosure]] adds 9,100-plus CoCo accounts, more than 2,000 in-quarter additions, 5,800 CoWork accounts, and an explicit link from agent-product use to higher core consumption and broader AI revenue.
- [[wiki/events/2026-06-30 OpenRouter reports agentic tokens surpass human traffic|OpenRouter's request logs]] show agentic keys using about 15 times more tokens per request than human keys and agentic token volume passing human volume around February 1. The provider-specific classifier and absent user, task, and outcome denominators prevent a market-wide conclusion.
- [[wiki/events/2026-10-14 G2 finds agent purchasing and production rise with oversight costs|G2's July survey]] finds 66% of agent buyers in limited or scaled production and 38% of production organisations operating more than 100 agents, supporting fleet-depth expansion beyond isolated pilots.
- [[wiki/events/2026-10-26 Bank and FCA measure agent adoption and bounded autonomy|The Bank of England and FCA survey]] finds 80% of full-survey respondents with agents deployed, but only 2% reporting broad autonomy. It shows that access and deployment have spread faster than deep delegation, while the top ten firms' concentration of wider AI systems suggests uneven organisational depth.

## Challenging evidence

- [[wiki/events/2026-07-23 Google publishes ATLAS v1]] finds broad but shallow use and little end-to-end automation on its observed mainstream surfaces, while explicitly excluding major enterprise and agentic products.
- OpenAI reports relative usage intensity without absolute firms, tasks, accepted outputs, or business outcomes.
- Microsoft reports an active-agent growth ratio without absolute counts; one day of user-initiated use or one autonomous run is sufficient for an agent to count.
- Cross-provider telemetry uses different products, units, populations, thresholds, and automation definitions. [[wiki/concepts/Measurement Surface Bias|Measurement Surface Bias]] prevents the ratios from being combined into a market-wide adoption rate.
- Frontier firms may be selected for pre-existing technical capability, data quality, budget, or organizational readiness; the public evidence does not establish that deeper agent use caused better performance.
- Uber does not disclose absolute users, total spend, accepted-change outcomes, review burden, quality trends, or net engineering savings; deep activity can still be economically shallow if requests do not improve completed-work economics.
- G2 also weakens an unqualified depth thesis: 47% of buyers still use informal or department-led controls, 31% report at least six hours of weekly oversight, and 46% report higher-than-expected operating costs. Its combined limited-or-scaled category does not show how much of the market has deep, organisation-wide delegation.

## What would change the thesis

- Repeated operator-side disclosures linking agent depth to accepted task volume, quality, correction, human-review effort, and economics would strengthen it.
- Absolute provider telemetry showing that the median is converging rapidly would weaken the separation claim.
- Evidence that high relative usage reflects experimentation, duplicate agent objects, or low-value message volume rather than durable workflow integration would weaken it.
