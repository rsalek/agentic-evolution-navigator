# Production Agent Economics

The end-to-end cost and value of completed agent work after latency, failures, supervision, security, and integration are included.

## Observable signals

- Cost per successful task, not token cost alone.
- Completion rate, latency, exception rate, and human-review time.
- Revenue per task, resolution, transaction, or attributed conversion.

## Current graph evidence

- Salesforce's VA release reports a three-year Agentic Enterprise License Agreement with a $1.6 billion total ceiling for Missionforce and related capabilities; this is contracted-revenue evidence, but the ceiling covers a broad stack and does not disclose exercised value or the agent-attributed share.
- Five9 reports an unnamed academic health-system expansion with more than $1.1 million in expected ARR and a separate healthcare commitment growing from about $6 million to more than $10 million over three years; neither disclosure isolates agent-attributed ARR or savings.
- Talkspace's 50% high-risk identification, 47% therapeutic-quality, and 3x satisfaction improvements are test metrics, not production unit economics.
- C Spire reports a 44% triage-time reduction, 125% more first-line closure, and more than 283 staff hours returned per month.
- Rossmann reports a 50% labour-cost reduction on AI-handled cases, while its EUR 20 million to EUR 30 million annual savings figure remains a projection.
- Singtel adds transaction and officer-avoidance volumes, while R1 adds early authentication-time and call-coverage metrics.
- Falabella adds a bounded autonomous-resolution denominator and NatWest adds a comparative resolution lift, but neither reports direct economics.
- MoneyHero reports simultaneous application growth and employee-cost reduction but does not isolate AI causality; Lufthansa's vendor-side savings claim lacks a published calculation; ixigo/TARA reports no direct economics.
- Delivery Hero reports engineer-equivalent output and weekly growth for Herogen without accepted-change, quality, review-effort, or financial-impact measures.
- OpenAI's 16x Codex message gap and Microsoft's 15x active-agent growth are directional intensity measures, not absolute task volume, outcomes, or unit economics.
- Microsoft's September 2025 management transcript reports about 12% higher throughput for its customer-service agents and says roughly half of realized savings came from AI deflection, but it gives no baseline, measurement window, dollar amount, or workforce coverage.
- The graph therefore has stronger service-level and labour evidence, but still lacks customer-audited cost per successful task, correction and recovery cost, agent-attributed margin, and durable multi-period savings.
- Cloudflare's Kitesurf release claims lower CPU and memory consumption than Chromium for common agentic tasks, but gives no benchmark values, workload denominator, customer cost, paid deployment, or agent-attributed revenue; it is directional infrastructure-economics evidence only.
- Salesforce reports $1.2 billion of Agentforce ARR, up 205% year over year, 3.8 billion production Agentic Work Units delivered to date, 111% quarter-over-quarter AWU growth, and more than 50% of combined Agentforce and Data 360 bookings from existing customers. This is the graph's clearest current agent-attributed recurring-revenue signal, but retention, customer concentration, realized revenue, margin, and correction cost remain open.
- KPMG's Q2 2026 survey finds only 26% of large U.S. organizations have full real-time AI cost visibility and 35% cite cost management as a barrier; deployment and orchestration are expanding, but the survey does not establish customer-level production economics.
- Cisco's controlled agent-task benchmark reports up to 450% more network traffic than a human equivalent, with approximately 70% of the agent-triggered traffic being AI inference. This establishes potential resource intensity and a network-capacity constraint, not cost per successful task, pricing, margin, or agent-attributed revenue.
- The Dageno-surfaced private-company set shows an earlier economics layer around agent discovery: Peec AI reports $4M+ ARR and AI-originated funnel attribution, Profound sells Agents by usage credits, Gauge prices a $599/month execution workflow, and Scrunch reported paid agent-facing delivery before its Sitecore acquisition. None discloses a clean agent-attributed margin, retention, or unit-cost bridge.
- Coinbase's Q2 2026 agentic-finance disclosure adds repeated quarter-level x402 usage and a published $0.001 facilitator price after the free tier, but no absolute volume, realized revenue, or margin bridge. Alipay AI Pay reports more than 300 million agent payments and more than 100 million users across 12 scenarios, but no period, transaction value, take rate, or agent-specific revenue. Both are emerging commercial signals with material measurement gaps.
- xpander.ai's public control-plane page exposes agent runs, agent registry, spend, approvals, policy, and cost observability as the intended product surface. It does not establish customer telemetry, paid deployment, pricing, or unit economics.
- TrueFoundry's TrueForge benchmark reports approximately 30% lower same-model cost per run than Claude Managed Agents and up to 75% lower cost with GLM-5.2 across 14 cross-system enterprise tasks at similar solved-task rates. The setup is unusually explicit and reproducible, but remains a company-run benchmark without independent replication, customer telemetry, managed attach, or TrueFoundry revenue evidence.
- Serval reports more than 90% existing-customer adoption of Catalyst after beta and a 50% faster workflow-building result at Ramp across 10 departments. This is a measurable operating-benefit signal at production maturity, but it lacks customer-side confirmation, a baseline protocol, task denominator, pricing, retention, margin, and agent-attributed revenue.
- ClickHouse's primary May disclosure reports more than $250 million of ARR, over three times the prior-year level, 4,000 customers, and more than 1,000 net additions since January. Original August reporting places ARR above $350 million, up 40% from May, and links demand to agent database and observability workloads, including roughly tenfold OpenAI usage growth. This is an emerging recurring-revenue bridge, but the agent-derived share of ARR, paid-service status of the cited workload, retention, concentration, and product-level margin remain undisclosed.
- Haizhi Technology reports RMB114.6 million of Atlas Agent revenue for the first half of 2026, up 135.6% year over year, with 29 customers, 38.8% of group revenue, 69% conversion among existing Atlas Graph customers, and a 52.9% segment gross margin. This is the graph's clearest listed-company product-line revenue and margin bridge after Salesforce, but recurring mix, backlog, retention, concentration, implementation cost, and cash conversion remain undisclosed.
- The Six Five comparison adds scaled internal use at Snowflake and named operating outcomes linked to MongoDB customer workloads, but neither establishes agent-attributed vendor revenue. Marvell's filed AI data-centre revenue is commercially real without agent attribution; HP, Intel, Oracle, and Dell add architecture or production testimony without bounded economics. Salesforce's separately filed Agentforce ARR remains the only measured agent-product revenue signal in this cohort.
- Salesforce's Q2 update reports more than $1.5 billion of Agentforce ARR, up over 240% year over year, 3.2 billion in-quarter production work units, and more-than-doubled premium-SKU bookings quarter over quarter. The metric now includes Slackbot and Headless 360, so sequential ARR growth is not cleanly comparable.
- Runable reports a $2 million annualized revenue run rate within three weeks of enabling payments and says 60% to 70% of one trillion tokens used over 90 days came from paying customers. It also reports negative gross margins from inference subsidies, making it a useful direct example of demand preceding durable unit economics.
- Workday reports more than 5,500 customers using at least one organic agent, up more than 35% quarter over quarter, while broader AI drove more than 25% of new ACV. The customer growth is agent-specific; the ACV attribution is not.
- Uber reports more than 30,000 daily agent-skill executions, 9.4-fold weekly request growth from February to August, relatively stable aggregate AI spend after April, a nearly 34% reduction in fixed-model cost per 1,000 requests, and a 52% reduction in cost per session from its June peak. The missing bridge is cost and quality per accepted outcome.
- ServiceNow reports more than $1 billion of AI ACV and ninefold growth in agentic deployments over nine months. The two measures establish contracted AI demand and agentic scaling in the same platform, but agent-specific ACV, paid deployment count, active use, renewal, and margin are not separated.
- Fingerprint reports more than $50 million of platform ARR, nearly 200% two-year revenue growth, 93% customer growth, more than one billion devices identified monthly, and more than 80 million API events daily. Its authorized-agent classification is production evidence, but the agent-specific share of revenue, customers, and traffic is not disclosed.
- AWS's OpenSearch Agent Health walkthrough makes pass rate, cost, and duration inspectable across evaluation runs, but its retail workflow uses simulated tool calls and reports no customer economics.
- Amber International reports $7.357 million of agentic-category revenue in Q2 2026, including $3.5 million from newly recognized A-MM Agentic Market Making. The category rose 62.2% year over year and 69.6% quarter over quarter, while A-MM contributed to gross-margin expansion; reclassification of legacy marketing revenue and absent product-level margin or renewal limit attribution.
- Atlassian has defined a direct outcome toll of $1.00 per successful autonomous service resolution, discounted to $0.85 at 200,000-plus monthly prepaid resolutions, plus $0.01 per excess Rovo credit. Billing starts December 3, 2026, so this is priced capacity rather than realized revenue.
- Snowflake reports a meaningful Q2 FY2027 AI-revenue step-up, says CoWork consumption is driving revenue and CoCo-using accounts consume more core platform, and attributes approximately half of growth acceleration to a broad AI portfolio that includes CoCo, CoWork, AI functions, and Gateway. More than 2,000 net new CoCo accounts and nearly 11% quarter-over-quarter CoWork account growth establish customer expansion, but agent-product revenue and margin remain undisclosed and fast-growing AI workloads currently carry lower contribution margins.
- Harness reports 500,000 agent CLI actions versus 300,000 human actions in the interface's first few weeks and more than 10,000 claimed internal AI-review hours saved in one month. This is scaled activity and directional operating benefit, not customer unit economics or monetization: the calculation method, accepted outcomes, quality adjustment, payer conversion, attach, revenue, retention, and margin are undisclosed.
- TRM Labs finds repeated likely-agent x402 payment value of only about $5,000 to $11,000 per month after bounding agency with on-chain heuristics. The rail has a pay-per-call mechanism and published facilitator pricing elsewhere in the graph, but merchant revenue, realized facilitator fees, take rate, active-agent breadth, retention, and margin remain unmeasured.
- Meta's Muse launch adds reported $20 and $100 monthly consumer tiers for additional agent usage. This is a direct pricing bridge at announcement maturity, not realized economics: paid conversion, retained task volume, transaction value, revenue, support and inference costs, and contribution margin are undisclosed.
- IBEX reports two strategic AI-agent new-logo wins in fiscal Q4 2026 and more than 10 completed implementations across five verticals. Broader Wave iX growth contributed to company revenue growth, but agent contract value, recognized revenue, active usage, renewal, outcomes, and margin remain undisclosed.
- TSA's Ace agent handles approximately 100,000 conversations per month, resolves 96% of routine inquiries without escalation, and is reported to have reduced interaction cost by more than 90% from a baseline above $4. The $11 million three-year labour value and 11,660 annual hours are projections; Salesforce contract value, fully loaded unit cost, quality-adjusted outcomes, and margin remain undisclosed.
- Datasea reports five agent-marketing customers receiving about $1.10 million of services by September 17, 2026, with a recharge-and-consumption billing model. The delivered amount is not recognized revenue; projected monthly fees and the $89.4 million annualized ceiling are nonbinding and may include non-agent marketing work.
- DataDome reports that 54% of its customers adopted agent-trust policies in Q2 while its commercial platform repeatedly classified billions of AI requests across customer sites. This is an emerging paid-monitoring bridge, but the company does not disclose incremental agent-trust pricing, contract expansion, ARR, renewal, customer outcomes, or margin.
- Snowflake's planned Agent Observability product proposes trace-level cost and business-outcome attribution. It could convert agent telemetry into storage, query, and monitoring consumption, but the private preview has no pricing, paid deployment, usage, or revenue evidence.
