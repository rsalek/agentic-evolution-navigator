# Six Five Summit 2026 agentic AI evidence comparison

Eight user-supplied Quartr transcripts from the Six Five Summit: AI Unleashed 2026 were reviewed as company-speaker testimony, then checked against filings, product documentation, named-customer material, and independent reporting where available. Exact filenames and hashes are retained in the local-only `raw/private/2026-08-26-six-five-summit-transcript-manifest.md`; full transcript text is not published.

## What the transcripts support

1. **Context and systems of record are the common control surface.** Salesforce, Snowflake, MongoDB, and Oracle all make governed enterprise data, semantics, permissions, and application state central to useful agents. This supports the incumbent-distribution thesis but does not prove that incumbents retain the orchestration or economic layer.
2. **Hybrid placement is an economic and governance response.** HP, Intel, Oracle, Dell, and Snowflake describe routing work among endpoints, customer-controlled infrastructure, cloud models, and specialist accelerators according to cost, privacy, latency, and capability.
3. **Governance becomes an operational dependency in production.** Dell's agent identity, logging, and kill-switch language; Snowflake's spending limits; Salesforce's governed application actions; and HP's endpoint controls converge on policy and intervention infrastructure.
4. **Agentic inference changes infrastructure requirements, but attribution is weak.** Intel and Marvell describe greater need for orchestration, memory, context movement, networking, and inference capacity. Marvell has genuine AI-infrastructure commercial traction, but no evidence isolates agents as the cause.
5. **Commercial proof remains sparse.** Salesforce's separately filed Agentforce ARR is the only measured agent-product revenue signal in this company set. Snowflake and MongoDB add operating-benefit or demand green shoots; Dell adds production testimony; HP, Intel, and Oracle mainly add readiness. None of the summit interviews supplies retention, margin, or customer-audited cost per successful task.

## Contradictions and corrections retained

- Salesforce says most customers remain near the beginning even while reporting rapid Agentforce ARR and work-unit growth; product-level scale and broad customer maturity are not the same claim.
- The Salesforce interview's 20,000 Dell **suppliers** conflicts with prior Salesforce descriptions of roughly 19,000 or 20,000 Dell **users** across more than 90 processes. The graph retains the earlier defined unit.
- MongoDB's transcript renders the AT&T metric as "$12 million of downtime saved." Independent reporting identifies 12 million **customer downtime hours**, plus 3.1 million avoided dispatches. No dollar saving is inferred.
- Marvell's interview says AI data-centre activity exceeds $2 billion quarterly; the last filed quarter reports $1.833 billion of total data-centre revenue. The newer run rate remains a management update awaiting the next results.
- HP and Intel stress ROI and efficient placement, but HP's quantitative savings and adoption figures lack disclosed methodology and Intel provides no named deployment economics.

## Decision-useful conclusion

The evidence supports an adoption stack, not a uniform commercialization wave: governed context and workflow integration enable production; identity, policy, and observability constrain it; heterogeneous compute and memory carry the workload; and only then can recurring software or infrastructure economics appear. For these eight companies, observable agent monetization is concentrated in Salesforce. MongoDB and Snowflake show credible early operating or demand signals without an attributable vendor-revenue bridge. Marvell shows scaled AI-infrastructure monetization without agent attribution. The remaining companies are better treated as architecture, readiness, or unquantified production evidence.

## Next verification triggers

- Salesforce: reconcile Casey and Dell units; track ARR, recognised revenue, renewal, expansion, margin, and customer-level outcomes.
- Snowflake: named external customers, active agent runs, paid attach, absolute savings, renewal, and Cortex consumption.
- Oracle: named agent workloads in distributed regions, active consumption, control outcomes, and paid attach.
- Dell: tasks, accepted changes, error/reversal rates, absolute savings, and customer-facing agent revenue.
- Intel: named production deployments, wall-clock cost-per-success benchmarks, agent workload share, and revenue attach.
- MongoDB: workload-attributed consumption, production retention, causal role in AT&T outcomes, and customer economics.
- Marvell: next-quarter validation of the above-$2B claim, agent workload share, customer concentration, and margin.
- HP: methodology for the 72% and savings claims, named deployments, realised outcomes, device/software attach, and revenue.
