# Dageno-surfaced private agentic company watchlist

Dageno was used as a discovery source, not as proof. Its July 9, 2026 comparison identifies Profound, AthenaHQ, Scrunch, Peec AI, and OtterlyAI among AI-visibility and agent-experience vendors; its separate Gauge comparison identifies Gauge as an agent-led optimization platform. Primary company pages were checked for product operation, pricing, customer or usage evidence, financing, and private-market access.

These companies are adjacent to the agent economy rather than direct agent-payment tollbooths. Their commercial mechanism is primarily monetizing the measurement, optimization, or delivery layer around AI-mediated discovery. The strongest current evidence is company-level SaaS monetization; agent-specific revenue attribution remains limited except where the product explicitly sells agent usage or attributes AI-originated funnel events.

## Additional private-market lead from the current graph

- **xpander.ai** — an enterprise control plane for agent registry, approved connectors and MCP servers, runtime and budget controls, audit, approvals, and deployment across cloud, VPC, or air-gapped environments. Its official platform page shows a production-oriented operating model and example run, agent, spend, and approval telemetry, but does not disclose paid customer count, pricing, ARR, funding, renewal, or margin. Keep it as a private early-stage diligence lead, not a green shoot.
- **MAGNE.AI** — an edge-AI hardware and on-chain infrastructure lead combining local agent execution, device identity, and programmable Agent Pay. The company-distributed financing release reports an additional $2.64M round and the official product page reports local runtime verification for an x402-compatible payment flow; MHash L2 testnet deployment is still pending, and no paid customer, shipment, repeated transaction, revenue, or investor-access evidence was found. Keep it as a low-priority speculative private watchlist lead, not a green shoot.
- **Natural** — a higher-priority private payment-infrastructure lead combining agent payments, credit, identity, authorization, risk, disputes, compliance, and settlement. Natural reports more than $40M of equity financing and a new $100M credit facility; its public credit product is still marked “Soon,” and no deployed credit, repeat payment volume, pricing, ARR, take rate, or secondary access was found. Keep it as a private-market diligence lead, not a green shoot.
- **TrueFoundry** — an adjacent private AI-platform lead. Its primary TrueForge benchmark reports reproducible cost advantages across 14 enterprise tasks, while its open-source harness and managed gateway create a possible distribution-to-monetization path. Pricing, paid managed adoption, ARR, funding, retention, and private-market access remain unverified.
- **Serval** — an AI-native service-management and automation platform. Its primary Catalyst GA announcement reports adoption by more than 90% of existing Serval customers after beta, plus a Ramp result of 50% faster workflow building across 10 departments. Keep it as a higher-priority private watchlist lead with an emerging operating-benefit green shoot, not direct agent-revenue proof: pricing, paid attach, ARR/ACV, retention, customer count, action volume, margin, funding, cap table, and secondary access remain unverified.
- **ClickHouse** — a late-stage private data and observability infrastructure company with the strongest new revenue-scale signal in this watchlist. ClickHouse officially reported more than $250 million ARR, over three times year over year, 4,000 customers, and more than 1,000 net additions since January as of May 2026. Original August reporting places ARR above $350 million, up 40% from May, and links growth to agent database and observability demand, including roughly tenfold OpenAI usage growth. Treat this as an emerging monetization green shoot with medium confidence: agent-specific ARR, paid product attach, retention, concentration, margin by product, and private-market access remain unproven.

## Evidence gate and exclusions

- **Peec AI:** qualifies as the strongest emerging private-market candidate because the company discloses ARR, customer onboarding, customer-growth velocity, paid plans, and AI-channel attribution tooling. The $4M+ ARR is company-reported and not isolated to agent-originated revenue.
- **Profound:** has the strongest scale and capital evidence, including a $1B valuation and enterprise distribution. Its Agent credits create a direct usage tollbooth, but the available evidence does not disclose Agent-specific ARR, retention, gross margin, or customer-level agent outcomes. It is a late-stage private secondary watch, not an early entry.
- **Gauge:** pricing and named customer outcome claims establish readiness and an emerging commercial mechanism. They do not establish customer count, ARR, retention, or causal revenue impact.
- **AthenaHQ:** its primary site provides operational telemetry and outcome claims, but no public pricing, ARR, customer count, or financing evidence. Treat the reported case-study outcomes as vendor evidence pending customer confirmation.
- **Scrunch:** its product and customer/funding evidence are material, but the Sitecore acquisition means the relevant equity exposure is now Sitecore or its owners, not Scrunch shares.
- **Writesonic, LLMrefs, and broad SEO/AI-writing tools:** retained as lower-priority Dageno discovery leads. They were not promoted because the reviewed material did not establish a distinct agentic tollbooth, agent-originated revenue bridge, or stronger private-market evidence than the ranked names.
- **MAGNE.AI:** financing and product validation are not commercial adoption. Verify legal entity, financing instrument, current device sales, paid deployments, MHash L2 launch, agent-payment volume/value, fees, and any qualified-investor access before considering private-market diligence.

## Next diligence triggers

For Peec AI, request current ARR composition, paid versus onboarded accounts, net retention, AI-originated funnel conversion, and whether MCP/agent features carry incremental pricing. For Profound, verify current secondary access, customer retention, Agent credit consumption, overage revenue, and agent-attributed ARR. For Gauge and AthenaHQ, seek paid-customer counts, plan-level revenue, cohort retention, and customer-confirmed outcomes. For Scrunch, map the Sitecore acquisition terms and any remaining standalone equity or earn-out exposure. For xpander.ai, verify legal entity, funding and cap table, pricing and contract structure, named production customers, active-agent and run denominators, renewal, gross margin, and customer-level cost or risk outcomes; treat the public dashboard numbers as product evidence until the company confirms they are customer telemetry. For MAGNE.AI, verify legal entity, financing instrument and cap table, device orders and shipments, commercial customers, network launch, transaction volume/value, fee capture, and qualified-investor access. For Natural, verify legal entity, facility terms, deployed credit, agent and merchant customers, payment volume/value, pricing, take rate, losses, disputes, retention, and qualified-investor access. For TrueFoundry, verify independent benchmark replication, production task volume, managed-gateway attach, pricing, paid customer count, ARR, retention, and margin capture. For Serval, verify legal entity, funding and cap table, Catalyst pricing and contract structure, paid attach, active customer and workflow denominators, renewal/expansion, task-level completion and correction rates, customer-confirmed Ramp economics, gross margin, and qualified-investor access. For ClickHouse, verify the reported August ARR with a primary update, the OpenAI paid-service status and contract value, agent-specific workload and revenue mix, managed-product attach, renewal and net retention, customer concentration, gross margin by product, legal share class, cap table, valuation, and any qualified-investor secondary access.
