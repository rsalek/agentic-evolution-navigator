Thesis evidence at a glance

What the signal supports, challenges, and leaves unresolved

Supports

  • Identified agent activity continues to expand.
  • Access controls increasingly distinguish agent traffic.
  • Readiness standards are becoming measurable.

Challenges

  • Referral value does not rise with crawl demand.
  • Readiness does not establish willingness to pay.
  • Traffic classification remains methodology-dependent.

Watch next

  • Paid access and attributable revenue.
  • Repeat agent-customer conversion.
  • Sustained changes across several regions.
ConfidenceMedium

Observed traffic and readiness signals are material, but commercial value remains unverified.

Why it matters

If agent-originated demand captures value without returning proportional referral or revenue, publisher leverage and the web’s economic model change.

What would change the thesis

  • Verified conversion or attributable revenue
  • Paid access or licensing at repeatable scale
  • Evidence that agents create net-new demand

Meaningful graph events only

Signal interpretation

Observed

In this prototype scenario, identified traffic rises while readiness improves and access friction stays elevated.

Graph-derived connection

These measurements connect traffic observability to agent trust infrastructure and production economics.

Inference

The ecosystem may be becoming more agent-readable before it becomes economically reciprocal.

Method boundary

Normalized trend — not absolute global usage.

Readiness exposes capabilities; it does not prove agent adoption or commercial value.

Promotion rule

Routine observations remain here. Verified regime changes are promoted to dated graph events with supporting and challenging evidence.
